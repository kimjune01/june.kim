Should an advertiser be able to say "match my topic tightly, but my audience broadly"? The math says yes. This post argues the auction should refuse the dial, because a bidding language should not sell a dial the bidder cannot set honestly.

[The paper](/power-diagram-auction) leaves anisotropic preferences as an open problem: elliptical reach instead of spherical, a covariance matrix instead of a single σ. The algebra survives the extension, since the score is still the log of the declared value, so VCG still carries. What it costs is the geometry: a bidder-specific covariance needs every quadratic feature lifted, on the order of d² dimensions, where the paper's mechanism needs one. But suppose the platform paid that. The report is still a covariance matrix over embedding dimensions, which asks the advertiser to declare which linear combinations of 384 axes matter more to their business, and nobody can answer, because [the coordinates mean nothing to a human](/buying-space-not-keywords). That is why advertisers [declare anchor text and the exchange embeds it](/relocation-fees). A bidding language is only as good as its worst author, and the worst author of a covariance matrix is every author.

Layers look like the fix. Decompose the position into named facets, each with its own space and its own reach: a topic layer, an audience layer. "Public speaking coaching for corporate executives" becomes two centers and two reaches, tight on the craft, tight on the customer. Each facet stays isotropic and declarable, the log score is a sum of per-facet parabolas, which is still a parabola, and the lift grows by one dimension per layer instead of d². As a mathematical object, layers are the anisotropic case solved: structure a human could in principle fill in, with the theorems intact.

As an ecological primitive, layers are wrong, and the failure is calibration. The per-facet reaches are declared, but nothing makes them mean anything. Is "corporate executives" twice as narrow as "public speaking," or five times? If the audience encoder's distances run at half the variance of the topic encoder's, equal declared reach is unequal economic breadth. Someone has to fix the facet ontology, the per-layer encoders, the distance normalization, the relative scales, and what a missing facet means, and every one of those choices moves money. The layered report is legible on its face and unidentifiable underneath: no advertiser's conversion data can tell them whether their audience reach should be 0.3 or 0.6 of their topic reach. The unintelligible math we threw out the front door walks back in through calibration.

The product also assumes something false. Summing per-facet parabolas means the conjunction is additive: match on topic plus match on audience. But "public speaking" is a different service for executives than for wedding toasts, with different rivals and different conversion behavior. The facets interact, and a facet product assumes they don't. A sentence encoder, trained on language where these facets already co-occur, can represent the interaction. The decomposition throws it away and then needs cross-terms to buy it back.

Against this, one embedding space is one habitat. Market positions are close or far, and everything downstream feeds on that relation: competition is adjacency, [territory is the cell around your flag](/power-diagrams-ad-auctions), and the diversity argument for the mechanism rests on every firm being measurable against every other. The coach for corporate executives and the coach for startup founders are neighboring businesses, same craft, adjacent customers, and embedding the two sentences puts them next to each other with no splicing. The encoder is opaque inside, but it is one artifact, open-weight and version-pinned, identical for every participant, and it carries zero per-bidder parameters. A layered market's metric is assembled from calibration choices that someone has to govern; the single space's metric is frozen in a hash.

So the primitive is the sentence. [Marketing-speak already fixed its format](/marketing-speak-is-the-protocol): what we do, for whom, in what situation. The encoder places the whole statement at the intersection of its facets, interactions included, and no one writes a matrix. This is the move the series keeps making, one level up each time: declare the language, let the machine do the math. Layers are how a theorist explains what the sentence did after the fact. The interface never sees them, and [the simulations](/relocation-fee-dividend) never needed them: firms in that market move as whole positions, flags drifting and territories carving, three numbers each.

The cost should be named, because it is real. A sentence cannot be tight on topic and loose on audience; one width covers the whole position. But one width is what campaign-scale evidence can estimate. A scalar reach is learnable from modest conversion data, per-facet reaches need far more, and a covariance matrix is hopeless for every advertiser who isn't a platform. Until conversion data can identify directional effects, isotropy is the honest default, and the series has been [removing](/one-shot-bidding) [parameters](/set-it-and-forget-it), not adding them. Expressiveness trades against clearing cost in the auction literature and against declaration cost in the market, and the declaration axis binds first.

Two primitives survive alongside the sentence. Serviceability is a filter: the pizzeria that delivers within five miles does not serve the sixth mile at reduced probability, so [delivery range enters upstream as eligibility](/market-position-json), and the paper proves filters preserve efficiency among the eligible. And separate businesses are separate positions, composing by OR, safe while filters keep the branches disjoint. Filters for possibility, the sentence for probability, positions for plurality.

The layer theorem is still worth proving, as insurance rather than product: if a platform ever does decompose facets, the block-isotropic lift says the guarantees survive the trip. But the market should not take the trip. Positions are close or far in one space, and every firm a sentence can describe gets measured against every other. That is the primitive.
